## Background
The feeder canal segment serving the regional distribution system has reached the end of its service life. Three alternatives were evaluated against cost, reliability, environmental impact, and schedule.

## Alternative 1: Rehabilitate in place
- Capital cost: 18.5 million dollars.
- Reliability: restores design flow; expected service life 25 years.
- Environmental impact: low; work stays within the existing canal footprint.
- Schedule: 14 months.
- Risk: limited construction window due to delivery season; a wet year could push completion.

## Alternative 2: Replace with a pipeline
- Capital cost: 41.0 million dollars.
- Reliability: highest; expected service life 50 years, lowest water loss.
- Environmental impact: moderate to high; new alignment crosses two sensitive habitat areas.
- Schedule: 30 months.
- Risk: permitting timeline for the habitat crossings is uncertain.

## Alternative 3: Hybrid (rehabilitate now, pipeline in 10 years)
- Capital cost: 22.0 million dollars near-term; pipeline deferred.
- Reliability: restores near-term flow; defers the long-term fix.
- Environmental impact: low near-term.
- Schedule: 14 months near-term.
- Risk: defers, does not remove, the long-term replacement cost and risk.

## Recommendation
Staff recommend **Alternative 1 (Rehabilitate in place)** as the near-term action: it restores reliability at the lowest capital cost and lowest environmental impact within a single delivery season, while a future study revisits full replacement when funding aligns.

## Evaluation criteria weighting (for reference)
Cost 35%, Reliability 30%, Environmental impact 20%, Schedule 15%.
